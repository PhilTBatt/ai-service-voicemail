# 022: Voicemail transcription, built but switched off

**Date:** 2026-11-11 · **Status:** accepted 2026-11-11 (Phil: "since it costs money, no, we won't add that yet. Build it though, add it all in, and just make a toggle so I can turn it off and on") · **BUILD_PLAN:** Steps 4 and 6 (the fallback voicemail and its text)

## The question

When the assistant can't answer (an outage, a paused or suspended business, the kill switch), the caller leaves a voicemail. The tradesperson gets a text and an email saying so, with a link to listen. Nobody reads the recording, so "I smell gas" left on it arrives as a routine text, and the voicemail greeting's new safety lines (2026-11-11) are the only thing standing between that caller and nothing. Transcribing the recording would give the voicemail the same summary and urgency as a call, including the emergency text.

It costs money, a few pence a voicemail, and the volume is unknown until there are customers. So: build the whole thing, switched off; a switch on the Admin page; and, beside it, what the last 30 days' voicemails would have cost, so the decision can be made on real numbers.

## How it works

- `system_settings.voicemail_transcription_on`, flipped from the Admin page (audited, like the kill switch). Off by default.
- When a fallback voicemail is stored (the recording callback, or reconciliation), and the switch is on, and the recording is two seconds or longer (Twilio doesn't transcribe shorter), a `transcribe-voicemail` job is queued in the same transaction. The "new voicemail" text and email are held 45 seconds for the transcript, so one message carries the summary. A call gets one text and one email, so a transcript that comes later than that shows on the call page and the dashboard only. Emergency texts never wait, and go however late the transcript is.
- The job asks Twilio to transcribe the recording, with the result to be POSTed to `/webhooks/twilio/transcription`, and records the transcription's ID and a cost line (the recording's length, priced per minute).
- The result is a JSON webhook, checked the way Twilio signs JSON (over the URL with the `bodySHA256` parameter Twilio adds, and the bytes), then matched to its voicemail by the ID: a result nothing asked for is dropped. The words are stored as the caller's one turn, and the post-call job runs as for a call, with a prompt that says it's a voicemail (nothing asked, read back or confirmed). A failure raises `voicemail-transcription-failed`, once; it isn't retried.
- The Admin page shows the price, the last 30 days' voicemails and what transcribing them would cost (each counted as whole minutes, rounded up: Twilio's page doesn't say how it rounds), and, once on, how many were asked for, done and failed, with the estimated spend. The Costs page gets a Transcription column.

**Confidence:** high in everything on our side (every piece has a test against the database, the stubbed API and the fake). Moderate on the first contact with Twilio's real API: the credentials, the configuration body's `participantDefaults` (documented for two-channel calls; a voicemail is one speaker, and Twilio says it infers the participants for its own recordings), and the result's signature. RUNBOOK section 18 says what to check the first time the switch goes on.

**Strongest case against:** building a paid feature nobody has asked for, with its first real run still to come. The counter: the gap it closes is a safety one, the cost is pence, and the switch means it costs nothing until Phil decides, on the numbers the page shows him.
