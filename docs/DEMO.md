# The demo line

A one-page guide for Ant. The demo line is a real AnswerMate number answered by the AI, set up as a pretend plumbing business called **Ant's Plumbing**. Give it to anyone you're talking to and let them ring it there and then.

**Demo number:** _[Phil fills this in once the number is live]_

## What to say

"Ring this number now. It's a plumber's line, and the plumber is out on a job. Pretend you're a customer: say what's wrong and see what happens."

If they ask what it is: "It's an AI that answers a tradesperson's phone when they can't. It takes the message properly, works out how urgent it is, and texts and emails the tradesperson straight away. It never pretends to be a person, and it tells every caller it's an AI at the start."

Keep it short. The call does the selling.

## What they can try

Any of these work well. Let them pick, or suggest one:

- **A normal job.** "My kitchen tap's dripping." It'll ask for their name, a number to call back on, their postcode, what the job is and how urgent it is. It reads the phone number back to check it.
- **An emergency.** "I can smell gas." The AI stops and gives the official safety message word for word, with the gas emergency number, then carries on taking the message, and you get a priority text while they're still on the line. Try it early in the call: the demo cuts off at 3 minutes without warning.
- **A leak.** "Water's coming through the ceiling." The AI tells them to turn the water off at the stopcock if it's safe to, and marks the call urgent. The text comes after the call, like any other.
- **Push for a price.** "How much will it cost?" It won't say, however hard they push. The plumber decides prices, not the AI.
- **Push for a time.** "Can you come tomorrow at nine?" It won't promise. It takes the message and the plumber calls back.
- **Ask if it's a robot.** It says yes, plainly.
- **Be awkward.** Talk over it, change the subject, go quiet. It copes.

Things that won't impress anyone: asking it general questions, trying to book a real plumber, or asking about businesses other than Ant's Plumbing. It's built to stay on topic.

## What you receive afterwards

Within about a minute of the call ending:

- **A text** to your mobile: how urgent it is, the caller's name, number and postcode, a two-line summary, a link to call them back, and a link to the call page.
- **An email** to the demo inbox with the same details plus the full transcript and a link to listen to the recording.

For a gas emergency, or anyone in danger, you get a short priority text first, while the caller is still on the line.

Show the prospect the text when it arrives. That's the moment it clicks: "this is what I'd get."

## The limits, so nothing surprises you

- Demo calls are **cut off at 3 minutes**, without a warning. The real service allows 8.
- Each phone can make **3 demo calls a day**. After that it hears a short "demo limit reached" message. Use a different phone, or try again tomorrow. Your own mobile, the one the texts go to, is never capped. Callers who withhold their number count as one phone: 3 a day between them. A call counts once it's finished and stored, a minute or two later, so a fourth call made straight after the third can still get through.
- Calls are recorded and transcribed, and the AI says so at the start. Don't let anyone give real personal details they'd rather not have on a recording. Made-up names and numbers are fine.
- Recordings are deleted after 30 days.

## If something goes wrong

- **It rang out or went to a plain voicemail.** The AI couldn't be reached. The caller can still leave a message, and the system picks it up. Tell Phil the time of the call.
- **The safety message didn't play for a gas call.** Tell Phil the time of the call straight away. That's the one thing that must never fail.
- **No text arrived.** Check the call page link in the email. If there's no email either, tell Phil the time of the call.

Phil can see every demo call, with its transcript, and fix anything the same day.
